The High School Science Fair Experience
December 2024

<aside>
When I started my science fair journey, I was so caught up in the idea of always winning. Looking back now, after finally achieving those once-ambitious dreams, I realize that’s not what science fairs are really about. It’s the bubbly feeling in your stomach and chatting with people who are, like, 80% just like you—that’s what makes it special.

If I could give anyone advice about science fairs, it would be this: **ENJOY THE EXPERIENCE**. Now that I’m a senior, I miss all the memories I made along the way. I’ll turn on songs like *"Party in the U.S.A."* and *"Like a G6"*—the ones they blasted at National JSHS—and I regret not soaking it all in more, instead of stressing about competition results and college apps.

Trust me. Science fairs are where you’ll meet the coolest game-changers and nerds in the world.

</aside>

# Illinois-Chicago JSHS (Sophomore Year)

My journey with the Junior Science and Humanities Symposium (JSHS) began during my sophomore year when I participated in the Illinois-Chicago regional competition. While I’ve done competitions like eCYBERMISSION (also hosted by the Army) in the past, I never competed against crazy “CURE BRAIN CANCER” type projects. In my defense, the project I presented is really personal. I spent literally months trying to record and decipher my family’s neural data to classify attention spans.

I know it’s not as impressive as half of the Oak Park and River Forest High School kid projects, but I was proud of my work! Well, my dad wasn’t. He was yelling at me 24/7 on the car ride back from Loyola-Chicago because I didn’t even qualify for the top 8 (oral presenters) 🥲. However, it gave me an opportunity to connect with a pretty cool upperclassman 😇.

# Illinois JSHS (Junior Year)

Building on the lessons learned from my sophomore year, I returned to JSHS during my junior year with renewed determination. There were some boundary changes, so everyone from IMSA had to drive 8 FREAKING HOURS to Carbondale to compete instead of just 30 minutes to the Chicago location.

But Illinois JSHS was genuinely so cool. I stayed there for several days (unlike Illinois-Chicago JSHS) and had so much fun meeting different people and touring around the research labs at Southern Illinois University. I ended up qualifying for NATIONAL JSHS with one of my friends from IMSA. I truly felt so freaking happy that day.

Thoughts: My dad said someone was trying to purposefully cough in the middle of my presentation to sabotage me but what do you guys think? 🤔

# National JSHS (Senior Year)

Advancing to the National JSHS competition was a dream come true. The event brought together the top young researchers from across the country, and the level of scientific discourse was extraordinary. I even met FREAKING RSI KIDS, ISEF TOP WINNERS, NATURE RESEARCH PUBLICATION PEOPLE, and literal science fair legends I’ve stalked-*cough* known about since the 3rd grade. Every where I went, there was a crowd of people matriculating to either MIT or Harvard. There was even a kid next to me who was like ”I’m only going to Brown. I know it’s not MIT like where [redacted] is going but whatever.” 😒 Shut up, you’re literally going to an incredible institution that I would die for.

Beyond the presentations, National JSHS offered unique experiences like laboratory tours, keynote speeches from leading scientists, and collaborative workshops. I formed friendships with students who shared my passion for research, and these connections have extended beyond the competition.

But I did not expect to actually win my category. For context, I was battling science geeks and was in the largest competing category that year. When the judge said, “In the Category of Math and Computer Science… Ryan Cho” I literally burst into tears and had to go to the bathroom afterwards to compose myself. I think I was the only person that cried on stage so that was really embarrassing.
