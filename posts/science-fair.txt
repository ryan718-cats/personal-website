The High School Science Fair Experience
December 2024

<aside>
When I started my science fair journey, I was honestly way too focused on winning. Looking back after finally reaching those once-ambitious goals, I realize that awards were only a tiny part of what made the experience special. It was the bubbly feeling before presenting and talking with people who were just as nerdy and curious as me.

If I could give anyone one piece of advice about science fairs, it would be this: **ENJOY THE EXPERIENCE**. As a senior, I found myself missing all the memories I made along the way. Songs like *"Party in the U.S.A."* and *"Like a G6,"* which they blasted at National JSHS, still remind me to enjoy the moment instead of stressing only about results and college applications.

Science fairs are where you’ll meet some of the coolest game-changers and science nerds in the world.

</aside>

# Illinois-Chicago JSHS (Sophomore Year)

My JSHS journey began during sophomore year at the Illinois-Chicago regional competition. I had done programs like eCYBERMISSION before, but I had never presented alongside biomedical and engineering projects at this level. My own project was really personal: I spent months recording and analyzing EEG data from family members to explore whether I could classify attention states.

I didn’t qualify for the final group of oral presenters 🥲, but I was still proud of the project and learned so much from presenting it. The competition also helped me connect with a pretty cool upperclassman who showed me more of the science-fair world.

# Illinois JSHS (Junior Year)

Building on everything I learned sophomore year, I came back during junior year much more determined. Regional boundary changes meant that our IMSA group had to travel EIGHT HOURS to Carbondale instead of competing at the much closer Chicago location.

Illinois JSHS was genuinely so cool. Over several days, I met students from across the state, toured research labs at Southern Illinois University, and had way more fun than I expected. At the end, a friend from IMSA and I both qualified for NATIONAL JSHS. I was so happy that day.

Watching the presentation again reminds me how much my confidence as a scientific communicator grew throughout this process.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pwx_f3YRxio&feature=youtu.be

# National JSHS (Senior Year)

Advancing to National JSHS was a dream come true. The event brought together incredible young researchers from across the country—including students whose work I had known about for years. I was meeting ISEF winners, published researchers, and literal science-fair legends. Their projects and conversations showed me how many different paths can lead to meaningful scientific work.

Beyond the presentations, National JSHS had laboratory tours, keynote speeches, and collaborative workshops. More importantly, I made friends who were just as excited about research as I was, and several of those friendships continued after the competition.

I genuinely did not expect to win my category, especially because Math and Computer Science was the largest category that year. When the judge announced, “In the Category of Math and Computer Science… Ryan Cho,” I literally burst into tears and had to compose myself afterward. Slightly embarrassing? Yes. But it was also the emotional conclusion to years of experimenting, revising, presenting, and learning.
