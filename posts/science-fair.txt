The High School Science Fair Experience
December 2024

<aside>
When I started my science fair journey, I was focused almost entirely on winning. Looking back after eventually reaching the goals I had set for myself, I realize that awards were only a small part of what made the experience meaningful. The excitement of presenting an idea and meeting students who were just as curious as I was mattered much more.

If I could give anyone one piece of advice about science fairs, it would be this: **enjoy the experience**. As a senior, I found myself missing the memories I made along the way. Songs like *"Party in the U.S.A."* and *"Like a G6,"* which played at National JSHS, still remind me to appreciate the moment instead of worrying only about results and college applications.

Science fairs introduced me to some of the most inventive and motivated students I have ever met.

</aside>

# Illinois-Chicago JSHS (Sophomore Year)

My journey with the Junior Science and Humanities Symposium (JSHS) began during my sophomore year at the Illinois-Chicago regional competition. Although I had participated in programs such as eCYBERMISSION, I had never presented alongside such ambitious biomedical and engineering projects. My own project was deeply personal: I spent months recording and analyzing EEG data from family members to explore the classification of attention states.

I did not qualify for the final group of oral presenters, but I was proud of the project and learned a great deal from presenting it. The competition also gave me an opportunity to connect with an upperclassman who helped me better understand the science-fair community.

# Illinois JSHS (Junior Year)

Building on the lessons from my sophomore year, I returned to JSHS during my junior year with renewed determination. Regional boundary changes meant that our IMSA group traveled eight hours to Carbondale instead of competing at the much closer Chicago location.

Illinois JSHS became one of my favorite high-school experiences. Over several days, I met students from across the state and toured research laboratories at Southern Illinois University. At the end of the event, a friend from IMSA and I both qualified for National JSHS.

Watching the presentation again reminds me how much my confidence as a scientific communicator grew throughout this process.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pwx_f3YRxio&feature=youtu.be

# National JSHS (Senior Year)

Advancing to National JSHS was a dream come true. The event brought together exceptional young researchers from across the country, including students whose work I had followed for years. Their projects and conversations showed me how many different paths can lead to meaningful scientific work.

Beyond the presentations, National JSHS offered laboratory tours, keynote speeches from leading scientists, and collaborative workshops. I formed friendships with students who shared my passion for research, and several of those connections continued after the competition.

I did not expect to win my category, especially because Math and Computer Science was the largest category that year. When the judge announced my name, I burst into tears. It was an emotional conclusion to years of experimenting, revising, presenting, and learning.
