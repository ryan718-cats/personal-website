Reflection on my Gen Chem Nightmare
Feb 2026

This was easily the hardest thermodynamics exam I had ever taken 😭. At least the struggle taught me a lot about how I prepare under pressure.

First: take a shower, eat early, arrive with time to spare, and please choose a desk that does not wobble. Sitting in the first row also makes it way easier to ask the teaching staff clarifying questions.

Second, actually study with the equation sheet instead of just staring at it before the exam. Print it, go through every equation, and practice recognizing when each one applies. Future Ryan, you will be happy you did.

Third, attend recitation and ask a lot of questions. I realized that free-response questions were not my main problem; multiple-choice questions were. The problem sets helped with calculations, but those conceptual questions required the kind of intuition you only build by actually participating and practicing more broadly.

Fourth, don’t be afraid to stay after class or go to office hours. Sit in the first row, lock in, and deal with confusion before it snowballs.

Also: PACE YOURSELF. Finishing the whole thing in 30 minutes is not the goal. You have time. Slowing down would have saved me from some extremely avoidable mistakes.

And write everything out! Use the blank pages on the back if you need to. One of my easiest mistakes on the exam happened because I tried to do too much in my head, which was honestly painful to realize afterward.

My goal for the next exam was simple: prepare more deliberately, participate more actively, and seriously lock in.

UPDATE: I finished General Chemistry II with an A 😄. I got an 80% on this exam—which was still one standard deviation above the class average—and learned that one bad-feeling exam is not, in fact, the end of the world.
