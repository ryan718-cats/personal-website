Reflection on my Gen Chem Nightmare
Feb 2026

This was easily the hardest thermodynamics exam I had taken, and it taught me a lot about how I prepare under pressure.

First, take care of the basics: eat early, arrive with time to spare, and choose a desk that does not wobble. Sitting near the front also makes it easier to ask the teaching staff clarifying questions when appropriate.

Second, study with the equation sheet instead of simply reading it before the exam. Print it, work through every equation, and practice recognizing when each one applies.

Third, attend recitation and ask questions. I realized that free-response questions were not my main weakness; multiple-choice questions were. The recitation problem sets helped with calculations, but conceptual questions required a deeper intuition that came from active participation and broader practice.

Fourth, do not be afraid to stay after class or attend office hours. Moving to the first row and engaging more directly made it easier to stay focused and address confusion early.

Pacing matters too. Finishing as quickly as possible is not the goal; using the available time carefully is. Slowing down would have helped me catch avoidable mistakes.

Finally, write everything out. Using the blank pages for intermediate work makes it much easier to check assumptions and identify where an answer went wrong. One of my simplest mistakes on the exam happened because I tried to solve too much in my head.

My goal for the next exam was simple: prepare more deliberately, participate more actively, and turn frustration into a better study process.

Update: I finished General Chemistry II with an A. I earned an 80% on this exam, which was still one standard deviation above the class average, and the experience ultimately made me a more thoughtful student.
