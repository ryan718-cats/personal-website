The Art of YOLO
October 2026

At the ripe age of 19, I’m learning to spend less time worrying about side comments and more time doing the things that genuinely interest me.

Being yourself can be difficult. It is easy to fall into the habit of performing a version of yourself that feels easier for other people to accept.

Working as a CNA in a surgical rehabilitation center has changed how I think about time. Caring for people during vulnerable moments has reminded me that life is both precious and unpredictable. It has made me more willing to try unfamiliar things, appreciate the people around me, and stop treating every decision as something that needs to be perfectly optimized.

For the first time in my life, I’m starting to let go of some of that rigidity. I went on a four-day hiking trip over fall break and focused on enjoying nature and being present. I started dancing to K-Pop and even tried breakdancing, although I’m definitely still learning. I tried rushing LOTUS; it did not work out, but I’m glad I had the experience and met a close friend through it. I also joined the taekwondo demonstration team after some encouragement from a friend.

To me, the art of YOLO is not about being reckless. It is about accepting that time is limited, choosing experiences over hesitation, and becoming more comfortable with the person I already am.
