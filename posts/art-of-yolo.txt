The Art of YOLO
October 2026

I’m too tired and old (ripe age of 19, by the way 🤣) to keep worrying about every side comment people might make about the things I want to do.

It’s hard being yourself sometimes. I think a lot of us fall into the rabbit hole of trying to be someone we’re not just so we can fit in.

Working as a CNA in a surgical rehabilitation center has really changed how I look at life. During one shift, I helped care for a patient who was facing a terminal illness. I’m leaving out and changing personal details for their privacy, but it was clear that the tiny inconveniences I usually stress about did not matter much in that room. A CNA beside me quietly said, “Wow, they’re around our age. It really makes you appreciate life a little more.” That stuck with me. It’s not really until you see how fragile life can be that you realize how precious all of these random little experiences are.

For the first time in my life, I’m starting to let go of some of my rigid controls and just do things. I went on a four-day hiking trip over fall break and tried to enjoy nature instead of overthinking everything. I started dancing to K-Pop and even tried breakdancing (I’m definitely not the greatest at it). I tried rushing LOTUS 😅—unsuccessfully—but I’m still glad I did it because I met a close friend. I also joined the taekwondo demonstration team after a friend convinced me.

To me, the art of YOLO isn’t about being reckless. It’s about remembering that time is limited, choosing experiences over hesitation, and slowly becoming more comfortable with the person I already am.
